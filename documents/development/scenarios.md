## Active Development Scenario

### Grossdeutschland at Butovo-Gertsovka (July 4-5, 1943)

**Status:** Primary development scenario
**Scale:** 200 meters per hex
**Map Size:** ~50x40 hexes (10km x 8km)

#### Historical Context

On the evening of July 4, 1943, German infantry launched attacks to seize high ground for artillery observation posts prior to the main Operation Citadel assault. By 16:00 on July 5, elements of Panzergrenadier Division Grossdeutschland, along with 3rd and 11th Panzer Divisions on the flanks, had seized the village of Butovo and proceeded to capture Gertsovka before midnight.

#### Sector Details

- **Location:** Southern Face of Kursk salient, XLVIII Panzer Corps zone
- **Front:** ~2 mile (3.2km) attack frontage

#### German Forces

**Panzergrenadier Division Grossdeutschland**
- 384 tanks total:
  - 15 Tiger I heavy tanks (heavy company)
  - 200 Panther medium tanks (new to combat)
  - Panzer III and IV medium tanks
- Panzerfusilier Regiment (left wing)
- Panzergrenadier Regiment (right wing)
- Divisional artillery
- Engineer battalions

**Supporting Units**
- Elements of 3rd Panzer Division (left flank)
- Elements of 11th Panzer Division (right flank)

#### Soviet Forces

- Elements of 6th Guards Army
- Prepared defensive positions (3 belts)
- Anti-tank guns on slopes
- Extensive minefields
- Artillery support

#### Terrain Features

- **Villages:** Butovo, Gertsovka, Cherkasskoye (objectives)
- **Waterways:** Berezovyy stream with marshy banks
- **Obstacles:** Extensive minefields (36 Panthers immobilized in one)
- **Elevation:** Rolling terrain with observation heights
- **Vegetation:** Wheat fields, scattered woods

#### Tactical Situation

1. **Left Wing (Panzerfusilier Regiment):**
   - Stalled in minefield early in attack
   - 36 Panthers immobilized
   - Subjected to Soviet anti-tank and artillery barrage
   - Engineers cleared paths under fire
   - Later halted by marshy ground around Berezovyy stream

2. **Right Wing (Panzergrenadier Regiment):**
   - Pushed through to Butovo successfully
   - Provided covering fire for left wing

3. **Key Challenges:**
   - Minefields
   - Marshy terrain
   - Fierce Soviet resistance
   - Mechanical breakdowns (Panthers)

#### Victory Conditions

- **German:** Capture Butovo, Gertsovka, and Cherkasskoye
- **Soviet:** Hold defensive lines, inflict maximum casualties

#### Why This Scenario

- Single elite division keeps unit count manageable
- Mixed terrain tests multiple game systems
- Combined arms: tanks, infantry, engineers
- Clear phased objectives
- Well-documented historical sources
- Minefields and obstacles test special rules
- 200m scale allows tactical detail while keeping map reasonable

- [War History - Grossdeutschland Panzer Grenadier Division – 5 July 1943](https://warhistory.org/@msw/article/grossdeutschland-panzer-grenadier-division-5-july-1943)

## Future Scenarios

### Battle for Ponyri (July 5-6, 1943)

**Status:** Planned
**Scale:** 200m per hex (recommended)
**Map Size:** ~40x30 hexes (8km x 6km)

#### Historical Context

Ponyri was a heavily fortified small town in the second Soviet defensive belt on the northern face of the Kursk salient. The battle became known as "mini-Stalingrad" due to intense house-to-house fighting. Control of the town changed hands multiple times.

#### Forces

**German:**
- 292nd Infantry Division
- 86th Infantry Division
- Elements of 9th Panzer Division
- Part of XLI Panzer Corps (Model's 9th Army)

**Soviet:**
- 307th Rifle Division (29th Rifle Corps)
- Fortified positions

#### Key Features

- Infantry-heavy urban assault
- Hill 253.5 as key terrain
- Fortifications and bunkers
- Back-and-forth combat (tests counterattack mechanics)
- Urban combat rules

#### Sources

- [Walking the Battlefields - Objective Ponyri](https://www.walkingthebattlefields.com/2016/11/objective-ponyri-battle-of-kursk-july.html)

### III Panzer Corps Donets Crossing (July 5, 1943)

**Status:** Planned
**Scale:** 500m per hex (recommended for river crossing scope)
**Map Size:** ~24x16 hexes (12km x 8km)

#### Historical Context

Army Detachment Kempf's III Panzer Corps under General Hermann Breith attacked across the Northern Donets River on the eastern flank. The crossing faced numerous challenges: collapsing bridges, traffic jams, and even friendly fire from German aircraft. Tigers were too heavy for some bridges.

#### Forces

**German:**
- 6th Panzer Division
- 7th Panzer Division
- 19th Panzer Division
- 168th Infantry Division
- 503rd Heavy Tank Battalion (45 Tigers, split among divisions)
- Corps commanded by General Hermann Breith

**Soviet:**
- Elements of 7th Guards Army
- Prepared river defenses
- Artillery positions

#### Key Features

- River crossing assault
- Bridge limitations (weight restrictions)
- Combined arms sequencing (infantry first, armor follows)
- Heavy Soviet artillery bombardment
- Minefields after crossing
- Tests amphibious/river crossing rules

#### Sources

- [Warfare History Network - Last Lunge in the East](https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/article/last-lunge-in-the-east/)
- [Wikipedia - Battle of Kursk Order of Battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk_order_of_battle)

### Olkhovatka Heights (July 5-6, 1943)

**Status:** Planned
**Scale:** 200m per hex (recommended)
**Map Size:** ~40x30 hexes (8km x 6km)

#### Historical Context

Olkhovatka sat on high ground providing clear observation of much of the northern front. The XLVII Panzer Corps attacked the 17th Guards Rifle Corps entrenched around the village. Despite Tiger tank support, German attacks were "broken on the slopes with heavy losses."

#### Forces

**German:**
- 2nd Panzer Division
- 6th Infantry Division
- 505th Heavy Tank Battalion (Tigers)
- Part of XLVII Panzer Corps

**Soviet:**
- 17th Guards Rifle Corps
- Entrenched defensive positions

#### Key Features

- High ground assault
- Elevation and line-of-sight critical
- Tiger tanks in action
- Demonstrates defensive terrain advantages
- Tests elevation combat modifiers

#### Sources

- [War History - Breaking into the Northern Kursk Salient](https://warhistory.org/@msw/article/breaking-into-the-northern-kursk-salient-iii)

### II SS Panzer Corps at Prokhorovka (July 10-12, 1943)

**Status:** Planned (large scenario)
**Scale:** 500m per hex (recommended)
**Map Size:** ~60x40 hexes (30km x 20km)

#### Historical Context

The climactic tank battle of Operation Citadel. Three SS Panzergrenadier divisions (Leibstandarte, Das Reich, Totenkopf) advanced on Prokhorovka, culminating in the famous July 12 engagement. Recent scholarship has revised casualty figures significantly.

#### Forces

**German (II SS Panzer Corps):**
- 1st SS Panzergrenadier Division "Leibstandarte Adolf Hitler" (11,257 men)
- 2nd SS Panzergrenadier Division "Das Reich" (9,443 men)
- 3rd SS Panzergrenadier Division "Totenkopf" (9,203 men)
- Corps troops (4,215 men)
- 232 operational tanks on morning of July 12

**Soviet:**
- 5th Guards Tank Army
- Multiple tank and mechanized corps

#### Key Features

- Large-scale armored engagement
- Multiple divisions per side
- Tests large battle performance
- Famous historical battle
- Complex terrain: Psel River, hills, villages

#### Sources

- [Wikipedia - Battle of Prokhorovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Prokhorovka)
- [Wikipedia - II SS Panzer Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_SS_Panzer_Corps)
- [Taylor & Francis - Armoured Losses of II SS Panzer Korps](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/16161262.2021.1889278)
